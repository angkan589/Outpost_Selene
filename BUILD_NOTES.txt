NASA science provenance: NASA LRO illumination mapping informs the simplified lunar light/shadow scenario; the published Curiosity RAD value (~210 µGy/day) is used as a Mars surface-environment reference counter; NASA Veggie and BVAD inform the crop and life-support context. No live NASA API feed or operational forecast is integrated. The in-game Science Desk states this explicitly.

Authentication: local-browser demo only; not server-backed or cloud-synced.


v3.1 celestial and surface-navigation upgrade:
- JPL approximate planetary orbital elements (1800–2050) plus IAU Moon/Mars rotation models calculate local azimuth/altitude.
- Moon sky includes Earth and Mercury–Neptune; Earth remains near its fixed lunar-sky region.
- Mars sky includes Earth, Mercury, Venus, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Phobos and Deimos.
- Sun position and local sunrise/sunset update when simulation time advances. Mars follows the 24.66-hour sol; lunar sky follows the approximately 29.53-day solar cycle.
- Scene Inspector lists each modeled body's altitude, azimuth and horizon status. Planet markers are deliberately enlarged for learning.
- Walk Around adds first-person surface movement with WASD/arrow keys and an on-screen movement pad; drag to look. Orbit mode supports Shift+drag pan.
- Moon terrain retains crater features; Mars has distinct red dunes and mesas.
- Astronomical sky is a JPL/IAU educational approximation, not a live JPL Horizons feed. Moon battery gameplay retains its separate 168-hour training illumination schedule.
- Validation: 18/18 tests passed; 54/54 seeded full-mission simulations passed. Syntax checks passed. Vite build remains to be confirmed after npm ci on the Mac.

v3.5 final planetary scene correction:
- Removed the artificial low-sky relocation of tracked planets. Selected bodies stay at their calculated azimuth/altitude; the camera remains fixed on the outpost.
- Track Body now applies a modest 1.35× highlight and a camera-facing tracking ring. Telescope zoom steps are gradual and capped at 3×.
- Mouse-wheel camera zoom is limited to 14–22 world units around the original 18-unit outpost framing.
- Rebuilt the local terrain curvature radius to 280 world units and the circular surface cap to 156 units, making the horizon curvature visible from the default camera.
- Added UV-mapped procedural Moon/Mars regolith textures, lunar impact-pit texture detail, Martian wind streaks, 280 instanced pebbles, and directional shadows.
- Re-anchored all surface structures, solar arrays, rover, crew, shelter/effect objects, job positions, and walking camera height to the curved terrain.
